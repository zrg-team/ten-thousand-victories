The Coronation
Vạn Thắng · Dragon Ascent · Design Dossier · The Return Run series

The Coronation

The first time the mode opens, the player makes their king — face, dress, name, house and banner — from the 298 wardrobe parts the game already owns. Zero new art: every option on every screen is a part, tint slot or emblem that already ships. A creator that doubles as the meta-progression's first lesson: what you make here is what grows across every reign.

01

The research — why a creator, and which kind

What the genre knows about made-by-hand identity

Three findings from games research, each with a design consequence:

  • Customized avatars measurably deepen engagement. The avatar-identification literature (Yee & Bailenson's Proteus-effect work and its successors) consistently finds players behave more investedly through characters they shaped. The IKEA effect compounds it: assembly itself creates attachment. A king the player built is a reason to come back that a rolled king is not — which is precisely the §Return-Run goal, "play again and again."
  • Named things trigger loss aversion and story-making. XCOM squads, RimWorld colonists and Dwarf Fortress dwarves all show the same pattern: the moment a unit carries a chosen name, its death becomes an event the player retells. Our Chronicle is a story engine waiting for exactly this — a king the player named is a king whose fall the annals can quote.
  • Lineage beats individual. Crusader Kings and Rogue Legacy demonstrate that in games where runs end, attachment survives best when it is vested in a house — the individual dies, the identity persists. Our Tông Phả dynasty ledger is already this shape; the creator is its missing front door.

一

Roll first, edit second

The creator opens on a finished, handsome king. Every control nudges; nothing starts blank. Skip is one tap and loses nothing.

二

Looks are free, numbers are not

Nothing in the creator touches stats, odds or economy. The wave shadow makes bought power a lie anyway — identity is the one thing safe to give.

三

The creator teaches the meta

Locked parts say what unlocks them. The rank-badge ladder is on the confirm screen. The first thing a player learns is that the house grows.

四

Ninety seconds, then war

Four steps, all skippable, defaults everywhere. A first-open creator that outstays its welcome costs the run it exists to serve.

02

What already exists — the creator is mostly built

Verified in-repo: the wardrobe is a part-picker API wearing a random-seed hat

The portrait system was built for procedural heroes, but its architecture is exactly a creator's: pools of parts, filtered by era/sex/office/rank, composited by a renderer that "knows nothing about heroes." The seed is the only thing choosing — replace the seed with a thumb and the creator exists.

The zero-new-art contract

The creator's whole inventory is what ships today, unchanged: the 298 parts in public/faces/ (heads, hair, hats, helms, robes, beards, marks, badges — six skin tones already one tinted file, so a skin stepper is free) · the tint slots the run already colours (skin, hair, robe, robeDark…) for the dress-colour choices · the CardIcons vocabulary for banner emblems · the ArmyRenderer kit for the march preview. Every locked row in §05 names a part that already exists (the 8 war helms, the gold coronet, the jade hairpins — all in the current families). If a design idea needs a new drawing, it is out of this feature's scope by definition — the doc's option counts are the shipped counts, not a wishlist.

The one rule that must survive the creator

The wardrobe's founding law — "the seed never decides who someone is" — was written because every named woman once rendered with a beard. The creator keeps the same shape: sex and era are chosen as identity on step one, and every subsequent stepper pages only within the pools those choices admit. A creator that let any part meet any part would un-fix the exact bug this system was built to fix, and break the era authenticity (the 1744 collar reform, the Trần cropped hair, bổ tử badges only on the round collar) that the wardrobe file documents line by line.

03

The flow — where it sits, and when it returns

"I'm the king. Not Nguyễn Hoàng." — the game already decided this

The strongest evidence for this feature is written in data/heroes.ts itself. The throne used to deal named emperors from a KINGS pool with temperaments, and a player's reported reaction killed it: "I'm the king. Not Nguyễn Hoàng." The devlog comment calls it a mistake worth leaving written down — "Whose dynasty this is was never the question. It is yours." The kings became Legendary champions who serve the throne; the temperaments were cut as display-only promises; and the run's king became generateKingHero() — a deliberately blank figure with a fixed name, sex: 'man' and no era.

The Coronation is the completion of that decision, not a reversal: the game refused to fabricate the king's identity, and this screen finally lets the player supply it. Consequently: the founder card needs no changes at all (it already offers champions only, and every harness answering options[0] is untouched), and there is no temperament step — resurrecting one would repeat the documented mistake. The reign's opening advantage is already a real card (openingOnly entries in ascentCards.ts) and stays where it is.

05

The progression hooks — what's locked, and what unlocking teaches

Locked wardrobe rows are the creator's second job: every 🔒 line names a system the player hasn't met yet, so the creator quietly indexes the whole game. The rule for what may be locked: ornament, never identity — sex, era, face, hair, a full court dress and the banner are all free from the first second. Locks are the flourishes.

Why locks belong in a creator at all

Cosmetic-unlock telegraphing is the best-understood retention device in live games because it is honest: nothing locked has power, so a lock is a promise rather than a paywall. Showing them greyed inside the creator — with the earn condition printed — converts the creator from a one-time screen into a place the player returns to after every milestone. The Temple (the re-dress door on the Tông Phả sheet) is where they come back to collect.

06

Integration spec

Small, because the wardrobe did the hard part years ago
// dynasty store (mandate:dynasty:v1) — extend DynastyFounder
founder?: {
  …existing…,
  givenName?: string,          // player-chosen; family name still derives the house
  look?: {                     // the creator's output — a stored HeroLook selection
    parts: Array<{ key: string; tint: FaceTintSlot }>,
    paletteSeed: number,       // skin/hair tones — the palette stays derived
  },
  banner?: { field: number; trim: number; emblem: string },   // the house's mark (UI chrome only)
  armyEra?: HeroEra,           // from the họ — styles the forces, never the king's dress
}
  1. New prompt kind coronation raised once, before mandate, gated on getDynasty().founder?.look === undefined — first ever open only. Terminal-style (drawn by hand like foundingCard, never through the decision director). Skip writes a rolled result, so the store's shape is identical either way.
  2. resolveHeroLook honours the explicit look. One early return: if the hero is the king and the dynasty carries a look, stack those parts; otherwise the seed path as today. The renderer stays ignorant, as designed. (The king is keyed by name, not id — the id is the constant 'king'; that trap is already documented in the heroes skill and both existing call sites.)
  3. Họ styles the army, and only the army — with an honest scope. With zero new art, "army style" concretely means two things: minted commanders take the họ's era (heroFactory already gives every generated champion an era and the portraits follow — the creator's armyEra simply biases that draw), and the banner opens on the dynasty's historical field colour. Garrison levies have no portraits and the battle screen's soldiers are era-agnostic — those render exactly as today, and the doc promises nothing about them. The king's dress stays decoupled: founder.era from step 1, founder.armyEra from the họ. No non-royal họ at launch.
  4. The creator pages the wardrobe's own pool functions (headwearFor, manHairFor/womanHairStylesFor, garmentsFor…) with step 1's era plus sex/rank — never a raw part list — so era law holds by construction. Each stepper tap re-renders the portrait via the existing renderHeroFaceInBox; faces are already baked per-look, so cost is one re-bake per tap, not per frame.
  5. Banner → the house's chrome only. A small drawn mark (field + trim + CardIcons emblem, baked once) stamped on the Tông Phả sheet, the ceremony screens and the war board header. ArmyRenderer is untouched — no map-flag system, no marching-column changes; the existing realm standard stays exactly as it is.
  6. Name entry is the composer (họ stepper + tên reroll from heroNames.ts components). The optional free-type path is a DOM overlay <input> — the game has none today, so it ships behind a capability check and the composer remains the path that always works (soft keyboards over a Phaser canvas are the classic mobile-web trap).
  7. The king override, at its one source. generateKingHero() hard-codes exactly the three identity fields the creator chooses — a fixed i18n name, sex: 'man', no era — so the integration is one read: if the dynasty carries a founder, the king takes its name, sex and era at generation. Both portrait paths honour the stored look: resolveHeroLook (which already keys the king by name, not the constant id 'king') and ui/dynastyPortrait's pseudo-hero for the Tông Phả sheet.
  8. The dress register and rank. The dress stepper offers two registers — court (minister pools: phốc đầu, viên lĩnh, bổ tử) and war (general pools: helms, armour robes) — which is the type parameter the pool functions already take; nothing new is invented. The rank parameter is dynastyRankRarity(level), which means the wardrobe visibly widens as the dynasty levels (rank-gated hats and gilt appear in the Temple) — that is the §05 unlock system for rank-gated parts, for free.
  9. Deed-gated unlocks live on the cabinet's deed list (grantDeed/deedDone, shipped in Phase 3) — "survive wave 10" and "first Jade" are new CABINET_DEEDS entries read by the picker; no new store.
  10. Prompt plumbing, the full checklist: the coronation kind joins PROMPT_PRIORITY (with the terminal-path kinds), promptSignature, the router, __ptOptions in playtest-lib, and the harness option maps — the muster card taught twice what happens to drivers when a kind is missing.
  11. The founder card is not touched. It already offers champions only (ascent.founderHeroId); the coronation precedes it and changes nothing about it.
  12. i18n: ~40–60 new keys (coronation.*), en + vi both, vi written not translated — the catalog header now says so in as many words.

Gates

Gate — verify-heroes.mjs still 15/15 (wardrobe + founder card contract untouched); a new verify-coronation.mjs: first boot raises it once · skip writes a complete rolled founder · the stored look renders (non-blank bake) · era pools refuse cross-era parts · the banner mark renders on the dynasty sheet · second boot never raises it.
Gate — shot-portraits.mjs contact sheet + /shot of all four steps on the phone aspect, and look at them — every wardrobe failure is visual.
Gate — smoke 15/15; modes-regression byte-identical (all of this is ascent + menu surface); tour still opens (the coronation must set the same first-run latch the guided tour reads, or the two first-open features race — the openPromptKey and tour-latch traps are documented from the guide round).

Traps, from the rounds that came before

  • The king's id is 'king': anything keyed on it is keyed on nothing — key the look on the dynasty store, resolve by name, exactly as heroBio already does.
  • Never bypass the pools: a raw part-list picker resurrects the bearded-women bug and breaks era law. The pool functions are the API.
  • One coronation, ever: gate on the store, not on a scene flag — an HMR dual instance of a scene flag re-raises it (the dual-module trap), the store does not.
  • Portrait re-bake per tap is fine; per frame is the perf ledger's cost floor. The stepper handler bakes once and stamps.
  • Prompt-kind shapes: add coronation to the harness option maps and playtest-lib's __ptOptions, or every driver wedges on the first screen of the game — the exact class of trap the muster card taught twice.

Effort: ~1–1.5 weeks, and none of it is art — the zero-new-art contract (§02) means build-faces.mjs and public/faces/ are not touched, `yarn faces:check` stays byte-identical, and the picker UI plus the coronation flow are the bulk; the render, storage, and map plumbing are reuse. Fits the series as its own small phase after the Hall (or before it — no dependency either way beyond Phase 2's dynasty store, long shipped).
