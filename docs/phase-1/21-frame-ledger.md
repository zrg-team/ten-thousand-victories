## The verdict

The game is CPU-bound on one thing: Phaser 4 re-tessellating hand-drawn ink every frame. Everything else — the paper shader at high tier, the beat rebuilds on the fight screen, the season turn — is a second-order hitch layered on that floor.

Vạn Thắng draws almost everything with `Phaser.GameObjects.Graphics`: hexes, settlements, soldiers, cards, buttons. That is the right call for a procedural Đông Hồ look, but in Phaser 4.2.1 a live `Graphics` is a *command list*, not retained geometry. Every frame the engine replays the list, runs `earcut` on every fill, rebuilds a quad per stroke segment, allocates fresh JavaScript arrays for all of it and uploads the result to the GPU with `bufferSubData`. The map already bakes its static layers to RenderTextures — that work cut the per-tick refresh by 70% in June — but what is still live is now the whole cost of a frame.

The numbers below were taken today against the running dev server (Phaser 4.2.1 confirmed), in headless Chromium for counts and CPU profiles, in headed Chromium for real-GPU frame times, at the 4× CPU throttle Chrome calls “mid-tier mobile”. Counts, commands, bytes and draw calls transfer to phones; milliseconds are this machine's and should be read as ratios.

1. #### Live ink is the frame. Settlements alone are ~60% of the map's geometry.

   On a fully revealed classic map, 29 visible `Graphics` replay 61,740 commands per frame; one settlement cluster is 26,806 commands by itself. Hiding the 3–4 settlement containers in view removes 66k→21k indices and 904→292 KB of upload (−68%), and ~35% of frame time. In Dragon Ascent the map in play is 85 visible Graphics and 155k commands — 2.3× the classic map — because the conquest world keeps more ink live above its bake.

   Evidence: §II tables; `FillPath.js:91` runs `Earcut` per fill per frame; `GraphicsWebGLRenderer.js:12–35` allocates a `Point` per vertex. Fix: §VI items 1–3.
2. #### The fight screen pays for its soldiers every frame and rebuilds its dock almost every beat.

   Each ink figure is ~1,150 commands (48 strokes through the three-pass `inkPath`, 7 fills). Two hosts of 2.4k/4k men are 125k commands per frame and 80–86% of the screen's render CPU; at 9k/12k men it is 435k commands and 49 ms per frame unthrottled. On the beat, `buildBattleOrders` is rebuilt on ~70% of beats because its signature includes last-beat losses and stamina — 17–26 Text and 25–73 Graphics destroyed and recreated, 65–106 text rasterisations — and the whole field (ground bake included) is built three times in the first five seconds of every fight.

   Evidence: §V.3; `measure-battle-beat.mjs` today: `updateBattle` 79 ms/beat, `buildBattleOrders` 30 ms/beat at 4×. Fix: §VI items 4–7.
3. #### High tier triples the geometry as well as the pixels.

   `pathDetailThreshold` is measured in *buffer* pixels, so at `RENDER_SCALE` 3 the same revealed map submits 136k indices instead of 68k and 1.86 MB instead of 0.93 MB per frame. Raising the threshold to 4 cut indices 33–50% with no visible change at scale 1 (screenshots diffed). Meanwhile the baked terrain stays at one design unit per texel, magnified 3× — so the high tier spends 9× the fill rate to show a map that is still 1× sharp.

   Evidence: §II GL table; `Config.js:392` default 1; `graphicsQuality.ts` tiers. Fix: §VI items 8–9.
4. #### Two full-screen paper passes per frame on medium/high.

   MapScene and UIScene each carry a `PaperFXController` as an external camera filter. In Phaser 4 that means each camera renders into a pooled framebuffer, runs the shader over the whole buffer (3 `valueNoise` = 12 `sin` per pixel) and composites back — 4 framebuffer binds per frame, ~15 MB of pooled FBO per camera at scale 3, and the canvas MSAA is lost for anything drawn through the filter. Headed A/B at scale 3: 24.8 ms with the filter vs 16.6 ms without.

   Evidence: §II headed table; `renderNodes/Camera.js:141,252–299`; `PaperFX.ts:42–77`. Fix: §VI item 10.
5. #### The season turn is the largest recurring hitch in real play, and no harness sees it.

   Every two ticks (11 s classic, 7 s Ascent) `rebakeScenery` repaints all scatter, rebuilds *every visible settlement node* (the season is in each node's signature, only to re-letter the label), and re-composites the whole static bake. Measured at 110–220 ms on 241 visible cells; on a revealed map the work is linear in cells and nodes, so expect 0.7–1.4 s at 4× throttle — in the same frame as the economy tick.

   Evidence: §V.1; `MapScene.ts:1959–1966, 2033`. Fix: §VI item 3.
6. #### Memory has doubled since June and the big buffers scale with tier, not with need.

   JS heap 25 → 52 MB on a fresh map, 114 MB revealed — 305 hidden bake-source Graphics retain 3.68 million commands so the bake can be redone. Two map RenderTextures are 52 MB at high (2244×3030 each), 29 MB at medium. 267 hero-face textures (7.9 MB) and a 1.6 MB QR code are uploaded at boot. iOS caps canvas memory at 224–384 MB per page and every `Text` at `resolution: 3` is a 9× canvas.

   Evidence: §II memory table; §IV.7. Fix: §VI items 13–14.
7. #### The harnesses cannot see the tiers they are meant to protect.

   Every perf script opens the page at `deviceScaleFactor: 1`, and `renderScale()` is capped by the device ratio — so medium and high (scale 2/3 plus the paper filter) have never been measured by any harness. `measure-render.mjs` and `measure-ui.mjs` hard-code port 5173, which is not this project's server. Headless Chromium rasterises in software, which charges fill rate to the CPU and hides GPU-bound regressions entirely.

   Evidence: §VII. Fix: §VI item 15.

## The engine: Phaser 4's cost model

What the 4.2.1 renderer actually does per object per frame, read from the vendored source rather than remembered from v3. Paths are under `node_modules/phaser/src/`.

### 1 · How a frame is rendered

`Game.step` → `WebGLRenderer.preRender` (clears colour + depth + stencil when `clearBeforeRender`) → per active scene, `CameraManager.render` filters the display list with `children.filter(child => child.willRender(camera))` — one array allocation per camera per frame, one `willRender` call per top-level object, *no bounds test* — and hands the survivors to the `Camera` render node.

The Camera node (`renderer/webgl/renderNodes/Camera.js:141`) decides `useFramebuffers = forceFramebuffer || forceComposite || internalFilters.length || externalFilters.length || alpha < 1`. Without that, the scene draws straight into the canvas through a scissor. With it — which is every camera wearing PaperFX — the whole scene is rendered into a pooled, canvas-sized framebuffer, the filter shader runs over it, and the result is composited back. WebGL1 framebuffers are never multisampled, so anything routed through a filtered camera also loses the canvas MSAA.

Objects are submitted to a **batch handler** by family: Quad (Image, Sprite, Text, BitmapText, RenderTexture, NineSlice, Particles), TileSprite, **Flat** (Graphics and Shapes), PointLight. Switching families flushes (`RenderNodeManager.setCurrentBatchNode`, `:395–409`), as does any `DrawingContext.use()`/`release()` — blend-mode change, filter, stencil, DynamicTexture draw. A texture change inside the Quad family does *not* flush the vertex upload but does start a new draw call. Buffers are `DYNAMIC_DRAW` and every flush is `gl.bufferSubData` into the same storage with no orphaning (`wrappers/WebGLBufferWrapper.js:200–227`).

### 2 · Cost per object type

#### Graphics — replayed and re-tessellated every frame

`GraphicsWebGLRenderer.js:110–429` walks the entire `commandBuffer` per render. Each `moveTo`/`arc` allocates a `new Path`, each vertex a `new Point` (`:12–35`). Every `FILL_PATH` → `FillPath.run`: transform every point to screen space, `Earcut()`, fresh `polygonCache`/`colors` arrays (`FillPath.js:63–91`). Every `STROKE_PATH` → one quad per segment plus a join quad per vertex when `lineWidth > 2`, fresh arrays (`StrokePath.js:63–198`). No geometry cache exists; `visible=false` skips all of it.

`fillCircle`/`strokeCircle`/`fillRoundedRect` are ~101-point arcs regardless of radius (`iterStep = 0.01`, `:230–288`; a rounded rect = 4 arcs ≈ 405 points). `fillRect` is two triangles with no earcut. `fillEllipse` takes a `smoothness` argument (default 32).

Escape hatches: `pathDetailThreshold` (per object or `render.pathDetailThreshold`, default 1 buffer px — prunes points closer than that in *screen* space); `generateTexture()` (Canvas-rasterised once, uploaded once); draw into a RenderTexture/DynamicTexture; or Shape objects, which earcut once per geometry change.

#### Text — a canvas per object, full re-upload per change

`setText` early-outs on an equal string (`text/Text.js:651`); `setFontSize`/`setFontStyle` are guarded too. **`setColor` is not** (`TextStyle.js:756–761`). Any real change runs `updateText()`: word-wrap via `measureText` per word, canvas resize/clear, `fillText` per line, then `canvasToTexture` → a full `gl.texImage2D` (no `texSubImage2D`). Canvas is `w·res × h·res`; this game sets `resolution: RENDER_SCALE` on every label, so a change at high tier rasterises and uploads 9× the pixels of the design-size label.

Render cost when unchanged is one quad — but each Text owns its own texture, so on phones (1 texture unit per batch, below) *every Text object is its own draw call*. BitmapText is quads from one atlas: no rasterisation, batches with everything else. The game has no BitmapText today.

docs.phaser.io/phaser/concepts/gameobjects/text: “This can be an expensive operation if used often, or with large quantities of Text objects.”

#### Container — per-child work, never culled

`ContainerWebGLRenderer.js:27–153`: one matrix per container, then per child per frame `willRender`, two `setScrollFactor`, two `setAlpha` (parent alpha multiplied in and restored) and the child's render step. Children are never bounds-tested. Interactive children cost a world-matrix walk per hit test (`InputManager.js:917–937`). Official docs: “The deeper you nest them, the more the cost escalates. This is especially true for input events.”

#### Image / Sprite — cheap, batched, but not culled either

Two matrix multiplies, `setQuad`, 112 bytes into the batch buffer. `alpha = 0` and `visible = false` both clear a render flag so the object is skipped before any render work, but it is still iterated. **Phaser 4 culls nothing but tilemap tiles and particle emitters** — `BaseCamera.cull()` exists (`:796–858`) and nothing in the renderer calls it. The project's `ViewIndex` is the only culling the map has.

#### RenderTexture / DynamicTexture

Drawing is command-buffered; `render()` flushes the current batch, binds the RT's own DrawingContext and renders the entries through the normal batchers — a bake costs exactly what drawing those objects to screen costs, once; displaying it is one quad. Memory = w·h·4 B colour + 1 B/px stencil while `render.stencil` is true. `setSize` does **not** clamp to `MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE` (`DynamicTexture.js:222–251`) — an oversize RT silently renders black. Contents are lost on context loss; the game already re-bakes on `webglcontextrestored`.

#### Filters, masks, Stencil

A camera filter = one pooled framebuffer + one full-screen pass per filter (`BaseFilterShader.run`, `:165–300`); padding grows the FBO; objects without bounds (Container, Graphics, Shape, Layer) get **full-camera-size** framebuffers. A Mask filter re-captures its source into a persistent canvas-sized DynamicTexture *every frame* (`filters/Mask.js:130, 208–241`) — the thing `RectClip` was written to avoid. A `Stencil` is two batch flushes plus the stencil geometry, no framebuffer (`stencil/Stencil.js:267–325`); with `stencilInvert` it also does a full-screen `FillCamera` write; `stencilCompositeCheck: 'auto'` walks the clipped subtree every frame to look for nested stencils (`:420–441`).

#### Tweens, timers, particles

`TweenManager.step` is linear over all tweens every frame; a tween whose GameObject target is destroyed self-completes on the next update (`TweenData.js:228`) — so the v3-era “tweens outlive their targets” trap is one frame wide on 4.2.1, though `onUpdate` on a plain `{t}` counter keeps running. `setVisible(false)` does not pause a tween. Particle emitters are the one ordinary object with view culling (`viewBounds` vs `camera.worldView`).

### 4 · Phaser-4-only tools worth knowing

- **DynamicTexture as an atlas** — `Texture.add(name, 0, x, y, w, h)` works on a DynamicTexture, so many procedural pieces (buttons, soldiers, settlement layers) can be baked into one texture and given frames: one batch entry even with one texture unit.
- **Shape objects** earcut once and replay cached vertices; a non-rounded `Rectangle` uses `FillRect` directly. A shape with no fill and no stroke passes through without breaking the batch.
- **Mesh2D** (4.2): textured triangles that batch with sprites — an alternative to Graphics for flat polygons whose topology is fixed.
- **SpriteGPULayer**: static instanced buffers, single texture, “modifying members is expensive”; some mobile WebViews fail to compile its sampler-array shader ([#7022](https://github.com/phaserjs/phaser/issues/7022)). Fit for a baked scatter layer, not for live ink.
- **`renderer.renderNodes.setDebug(true)`** + `debugToString()` records one frame's node tree — the built-in way to count batch runs and flushes; v4 has no draw-call counter otherwise.
- **Custom render nodes** (`render.renderNodes`, `setRenderNodeRole`): a custom `FillPath`/`StrokePath` could cache tessellation per Graphics keyed on command-buffer identity — the escape hatch if some ink must stay live and vector-crisp.

## The platform: phones, browsers, V8

The budget a mid-tier phone actually has, what its tiled GPU punishes, how V8 charges for allocation, and the memory ceilings that reload a Safari tab. Primary sources throughout; blog claims are marked.

### 1 · The budget

60 Hz is 16.7 ms, but web.dev's rendering guidance is that “all of your work needs to be completed inside 10 ms” because the browser keeps ~6 ms for compositing and input. Chrome's calibrated mid-tier preset came out at 3.6–3.7× on a reference desktop; Harry Roberts' 2025 reference devices are the Galaxy A15 (low, 9.1× slower than a MacBook) and A54 (mid, 2.9×). V8's own 2019 data: a median phone runs Reddit's JavaScript 3–4× slower than a Pixel 3, a low-end one 6×. So the project's 4× CPU throttle is the right proxy for the CPU — and Chrome warns that throttling *underestimates GPU-bound work by 2–3×* (a GPU-heavy interaction: 270 ms throttled, 620 ms on the real phone).

Chrome on Android generally runs rAF at the panel's active rate (90/120 Hz — community reports, not a spec guarantee), so on those phones the budget is 8.3–11 ms and Phaser's `setFPSLimit(60)` is a real lever. iOS Low Power Mode throttles rAF to 30 fps with no API to detect it (WebKit 168837, 215745) — measure the idle rAF interval; ≈33 ms means the budget is 33 ms. Frame pacing matters more than the mean: Android's frame-pacing docs describe how a 30-fps game on a 60 Hz panel becomes 49 / 16 / 33 ms and “short frames followed by long frames are perceived by the player as stuttering”. A locked 30 beats an unstable 45.

Long tasks (> 50 ms) and INP (good ≤ 200 ms) are the input-side metrics. A season turn of 0.7–1.4 s, or a 190 ms build-modal tap, is an INP failure on its own.

### 2 · The GPU: tiled, bandwidth-starved, allergic to full-screen passes

Every phone GPU — Mali, Adreno, Apple — is tile-based: geometry is binned, fragments are shaded into on-chip tile memory and written back once per render pass. Arm's GPU Best Practices Guide (101897) is blunt about what a 2D game does wrong: “Blending has a significant impact on performance because blending disables many of the important optimizations that remove fragment overdraw … The negative impact is especially noticeable for user interfaces and 2D games that use multiple layers of sprites.” Phaser draws everything blended in painter's order, so overdraw reduction has to come from drawing fewer, larger opaque layers — e.g. one baked ground instead of wash + cast + decoration + fog.

Framebuffer switches are the other tax: “Do not switch back and render to the same FBO multiple times in a frame … On Valhall, it is especially important to minimize FBO switching as the driver does a flush in glBindFramebuffer().” Each camera filter, RenderTexture draw and stencil pass is an FBO switch with a store and a reload. Pixels are quadratic in scale: 0.33 Mpx at scale 1, 1.32 at 2, 2.96 at 3 — and every full-screen pass (the paper shader, a clear, an FBO composite) is charged at that size. webglfundamentals: “it's a common optimization in games to actually render less pixels than are displayed and let the GPU scale them up.”

MSAA is cheap on Arm tilers (resolved in tile memory) but not free elsewhere; it only smooths geometry edges, so once vectors are baked it buys nothing. `alpha:false` is not a free win on some platforms (MDN: “the RGB back buffer may have to be emulated on top of an RGBA surface”). Use `mediump` in fragment shaders where precision allows (PaperFX already does). Avoid `discard` (late-Z) and anything that reads back (`readPixels`, snapshots).

Context loss is routine on phones: Chromium evicts the oldest context past **8 active contexts on Android** (16 elsewhere); iOS 17.0 lost contexts after backgrounding Safari until 17.1; memory pressure does it on both. The game's `webglcontextrestored` re-bake is the right shape; the fight screen's ground RT and every face badge RT need the same treatment.

### 3 · V8: shapes, numbers, garbage

Hidden classes and inline caches reward objects built in constructors with every field in the same order and punish `delete`, late-added fields and more than four shapes at a call site (megamorphic). Doubles are boxed `HeapNumber`s under pointer compression; a field initialised with `0` and later assigned `performance.now()` migrates its shape (V8's React-cliff post) — initialise hot floats as doubles or keep them in typed arrays. Arrays are one-way: a hole makes an array holey forever; mixing ints, doubles and objects degrades the elements kind.

Garbage is the game-relevant part. V8's young generation is at most 16 MiB; a scavenge is sub-millisecond only when almost everything is dead, and all pauses are 3–5× longer on a mid-tier phone. The Graphics path here allocates 12–16 MB per frame (§II.4) — that is a scavenge every one or two frames. Escape analysis removes temporaries only inside one inlined optimisation unit, so `{x, y}` results, spread, destructuring and `map/filter` callbacks in per-frame paths allocate unless inlined. Pool vectors (`curve.getPoint(t, out)`), reuse scratch arrays (`length = 0`), and keep per-frame strings out of `Text`.

`try/catch` is free under TurboFan; `Math.sin/cos` are fdlibm ports (slow), `sqrt/floor/abs/min/max` are single ops — compare squared distances instead of `Math.hypot`, which the scatter planner calls ~2.8 million times per repaint (§V.1). Map/Set beat objects for string keys; arrays beat both for dense integer ids.

### 4 · Text, fonts, input

Canvas `fillText` is the expensive primitive — a 2015 Firefox profile had 41% of a frame in it; caching strings into textures took the frame from 10 ms to 1 ms. Shadows and strokes multiply it (`setShadow(…, true, true)` on the map labels is two extra passes). `measureText` is a shaping call; cache per (font, string). Fonts are handled well here: `BootScene` awaits `document.fonts.load` for every weight before the first Text, and the faces are self-hosted, subset WOFF2 (~340 KB; the 600 weights, ~150 KB, are fetched but never used by any style).

Input: `touch-action: none` on the canvas and `width=device-width` remove the tap delay; Chrome aligns `pointermove` to one dispatch per frame; the map resolves taps arithmetically (`pixelToAxial`) rather than through per-hex interactive objects, which is correct. The only per-move waste is two DOM listeners (`pointermove` and `mousemove`) each calling `getBoundingClientRect()`.

### 5 · Memory ceilings

iOS: WebKit's own engineer puts the per-page maximum at “2–3 GB, it depends on the device model” *including GPU memory*, but jetsam (the kernel) is the binding limit on phones and is far lower on 2–3 GB devices — a blog-measured table puts practical ceilings at 300–400 MB on iPhone 8–12 (unverified, treat as pessimistic). Separately, iOS caps **total 2D canvas memory** per page at 224 MB (iOS 12) to 384 MB (iOS 15), 16.7 Mpx per canvas — and every Phaser `Text` is a 2D canvas. At `resolution: 3` a 300×40 label is 432 KB; the build modal's 102 Texts were 9.3 MB; 300 such labels would be 130 MB of the cap. Android has no documented per-tab number; the renderer is simply OOM-killed and an Android WebView “can't be reused” after `onRenderProcessGone`.

Hints are weak: `deviceMemory` is Chromium-only (powers of two, clamped 0.25–8); `hardwareConcurrency` is 4 on every iPhone 11–17; so `defaultGraphicsQuality()`'s `cores ≥ 8 && memory ≥ 8` can never pick “high” on iOS — which happens to be the safe outcome. The robust signal is the one the game does not yet use: measured frame time.

### 6 · Startup

Parse/compile is “2–5× as long on phones as on desktop”; a Moto G4 compiles ~170 kB/s of compressed JS. Top-level code and large object literals are eagerly compiled on the main thread at module evaluation — V8's advice for data ≥ 10 kB is literally to ship it as a JSON string and `JSON.parse` it (1.7× faster), or lazy-`import()` it. Service-worker-precached scripts get a *full* code cache at install (“compile everything and place it in the cache”), which is the strongest argument for the PWA precache the game already ships. SVG → texture is a synchronous main-thread rasterisation; 267 of them happen in PreloadScene today.

### 7 · The adaptive-quality pattern

PlayCanvas and Babylon both converge on the same loop: sample rAF deltas in 2–3 s windows after warm-up, judge by **p95 frame time** not the mean, step down one rung when p95 exceeds ~1.25× the budget for two consecutive windows (render scale 3 → 2 → 1.5 → 1, paper FX off, bake scale, scatter density, LOD), hysteresis before stepping up, persist the result, never decide in the first 5 s (JIT warm-up, shader compiles) and treat 33 ms as the budget when Low Power Mode is detected. The game has all the rungs already (`graphicsQuality.ts` tiers, `?nofx`, `bakescale`, `scatter`, LOD thresholds); it lacks only the sampler and a reload-free way to change `RENDER_SCALE`.

Bounded logs everywhere that matters (event log 100, chronicle 60, battle history 24); heap was flat over a 300-tick rendered run (54 → 60 MB).

**Loading — noted, not the priority.** The production build is one 4,208 KB chunk (1,147 KB gzip; +19% in two days), no `manualChunks`, no `import()` anywhere: Phaser's full ESM build is 1,685 KB raw / 381 KB gz (33%) and still contains Matter, tilemaps, video, lights and sound the game never uses (`phaser-core` would be 889 / 235 KB); the Chronicle's text is 679 / 217 KB, the catalogs 532 / 162 KB — 38% of the bundle is static content, both languages, all eager. Phaser also boots a `WebAudioSoundManager` (83–109 ms) for a game with no audio; the service worker precaches 4,983 KB including 533 KB of icons the page never fetches and downloads the bundle a second time on first visit. The native shell is Expo / React Native WebView (not Capacitor): on Android it sets `cacheEnabled={false}`, which is `LOAD_NO_CACHE` — the V8 code cache can never be produced, so every cold start re-parses the whole bundle — and `useKeepAwake()` holds the screen on for the entire session.

**Clock.** No `fps` config, so there is no frame cap (120 Hz phones render 120 frames per second for a sim that ticks every 3.5–5.5 s) and Phaser's `smoothDelta` replaces any frame over 200 ms with the previous sane delta — under sustained jank the game clock runs *slower* than wall time, stretching the effective tick on exactly the devices that are already behind. Phaser's visibility handler pauses the loop when the tab is hidden and does not catch up, which is the right behaviour for battery.

## Measuring honestly

The traps this investigation hit, so the next one doesn't — and the numbers that transfer from this machine to a phone versus the ones that do not.

#### Transferable vs local

**Transferable:** object, Graphics, Text and tween counts; command-buffer sizes; draw calls, indices and `bufferSubData` bytes per frame; allocation bytes per frame; listener counts; texture memory; the ratio between two runs that differ by one switch. **Local:** every millisecond. Headless Chromium rasterises through SwiftShader, so fill rate is charged to the CPU and full-screen passes look worse than they are on a phone GPU, while GPU-bound regressions can hide entirely. Run-to-run spread at 4× throttle is ±30%: interleave A/B runs in the same page (the `pathDetailThreshold` A/B in the appendix does this) and quote medians of interleaved rounds or min-of-N, never one pair.

#### Traps hit today

- **DPR 1 hides the tiers.** `renderScale()` is `min(profile.renderScale, devicePixelRatio)`, so a harness at `deviceScaleFactor: 1` always measures scale 1 with PaperFX off, whatever the stored quality. Set `deviceScaleFactor: 3` and `localStorage['mandate:graphics:v1']` in `addInitScript`.
- **Headed windows off-screen are occlusion-throttled** — the first headed batch reported 6 fps for a fresh map because the window was positioned at x = 2000. Keep the window visible.
- **`measure-render.mjs` and `measure-ui.mjs` hard-code 127.0.0.1:5173**, which is not this project's server (yarn dev is 5179; the perf skill suggests 5199). Both silently measure whatever answers there.
- **A fresh map is the cheapest the game ever is.** Three lands visible, no badges, no armies in contact. Every steady-state number must also be taken on the revealed map and on the Ascent map in play, which is 2.3× heavier than classic.
- **The Ascent founder overlay is not the Ascent map.** `__startBenchGame(seed, 'ascent')` lands on the founder pick with the map drawn beneath; resolve the prompt (the beat harness shows how) before measuring the map.
- **Sampling heap profiles report survivors by default** — pass `includeObjectsCollectedByMinorGC/MajorGC` or the per-frame garbage looks like 18 KB instead of 16 MB.
- **A realtime tick inside a 300-frame window** changes the answer (one settlement-bound run went from 4 visible clusters to 3 between rounds). Keep windows under `REALTIME_TICK_MS` or pin the accumulator.
- **The long-running dev server** can serve a stale optimised Phaser (the memory note) and emitted one `this.renderInstallMark is not a function` during this session — an HMR artefact, not a bug in the tree. Check `Phaser.VERSION` first.

#### On a phone

Android: `chrome://inspect` over USB (also the React Native WebView shell with `setWebContentsDebuggingEnabled`), Performance panel with no throttling, Perfetto for GPU/compositor traces. iOS: Safari Web Inspector Timelines → Frames view (30/60 fps reference lines), `isInspectable = true` on the WKWebView. In-page: a rAF-delta histogram reporting p50/p95/p99 and the share of frames over budget; `PerformanceObserver` on `long-animation-frame` (Chrome 123+, with script attribution) and `longtask`; `WEBGL_debug_renderer_info` to bucket Adreno/Mali generations and to detect SwiftShader in harnesses. Reference hardware: Galaxy A15 (low) and A54 (mid) per 2025 guidance, plus an iPhone 8/SE-class device for jetsam and the canvas cap.

#### Regression gates worth adding

- `listenerCount('state-changed') === 1` and `events.listenerCount('update')` flat after two runs.
- Indices and upload bytes per frame per screen, at DPR 3 / high: revealed map ≤ 30k, Ascent map ≤ 40k, fight ≤ 15k after the roadmap.
- Beat p50 at 4× ≤ 35 ms; Text creations on a quiet beat = 0; `buildBattleOrders` calls ≈ orders.
- Season turn on a revealed map ≤ 250 ms at 4×.
- No `(garbage collector)` sample > 10 ms during a scripted 60 s play; allocation < 2 MB per frame.
- JS heap after GC ≤ 80 MB revealed; total texture bytes ≤ 40 MB at high.
